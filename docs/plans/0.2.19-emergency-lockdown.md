# Emergency lockdown (0.2.19, decided; to be built)

`tacctl emergency lockdown` refuses the logins of chosen groups on every
device and Linux host of a scope. Superuser only. The decisions below are
the maintainer's answers to the design questions; the research is cited
from the code at `feature/0.2.19`.

## Verb

```
tacctl emergency lockdown <scope> enable|disable [<group>[,<group>...]]
                          [--tier <tier>] [--reason <text>] [--dry-run]
tacctl emergency lockdown --all-scopes enable|disable [<groups>]
tacctl emergency lockdown status [<scope>] [--json]     (bare <scope> = status)
```

- `emergency` is a family word (a later emergency verb joins it); no clash
  with `breakglass`, which stays as it is.
- Targets are store group names. `--tier <tier>` expands to the groups of
  that tier at the moment and records their names.
- `enable` with no list locks every non-superuser group; with a list only
  those; it is a union with what is locked already. `disable` with no list
  lifts everything, with a list only those groups.
- `--all-scopes` is confirmed by typing `all`, and the confirmation says the
  tacctl server's own logins are included.
- The scope's name is typed back at a terminal, no `--yes`, after the list of
  users who lose access and who keeps it (`--dry-run` prints the list).
- No expiry: it stands until `disable`, with the time since shown everywhere.

## Meaning

- A superuser is a user whose group's tier is superuser (`tier.ForGroup`),
  and the group is not ambiguous (a group at priv-lvl 15 with no tier
  setting is). Superusers are never locked. `enable` refuses when the
  caller's own group would be locked, and names the fix.
- Refusal: the locked groups' users are removed from the scope's user set
  at render time (`scopes:` of the TACACS+ user, the (user, scope) entries
  of RADIUS), so the server answers FAIL/Reject, never silence. New logins
  and every further command authorization are refused; open Junos, WTI and
  Linux sessions survive until they log in again or run sudo. Hosts of the
  scope are included, and so is the tacctl server's own console when the
  scope covers 127.0.0.1 (superusers keep it).
- It must not reuse the hold's carve (`conf.HeldAddresses`): silence makes a
  device fall back to its local accounts. `store.yaml` is untouched, so
  `disable` restores the rendered files byte for byte.
- Local accounts of a device are in no tacctl group. They are kept out only
  as a side effect of the device's method list (Cisco: the next method is
  tried on an error only; Junos tacacs-first renders no `password`; WTI
  falls back after transport failure). Not closed: the Cisco console list,
  Junos root on the console, local-first scopes (`local group`,
  `[ password tacplus ]`, WTI `On (All Failures)`), local ssh keys, open
  sessions, a device that cannot reach the server. `status` lists, per
  device, the residual local paths the templates imply, and `enable` on a
  local-first scope warns in red. Nothing is changed on any device.

## State and apply

- `tacctl.yaml`: `emergency.lockdown.<scope>.{groups,since,by,reason}`,
  validated like the hold's leaves, snapshotted by `StoreApply`, restored
  and named by `backup restore`, a decision in the rollback table, rollback
  refused while one stands, a warning in `config validate` (an unknown group
  is an error).
- One `StoreApply` under the file lock with a check (two superusers); the
  renderers apply a `model.WithoutMembers` transform beside `Carved` and the
  read-back compares against it. A render failure puts the file back; a
  restart failure keeps the record and says `NOT restarted`, exit 1.
- `scope rename` moves the key, `scope remove` drops it, `group remove`
  drops the group from every list with a warning, `breakglass hold` on a
  device of a locked scope is named in `status` (the hold re-enables local
  fallback there).
- Audit: `emergency lockdown enable|disable scope= groups= exempt= reason=
  by=` (disable: `held=<duration>`); the verb is `action()` in the audit
  table and has no `tier.Rules` row (superuser only).

## The message

One red bold line on stderr, once per process, from the helper that
resolves a scope (scope, device, host, config, user and group verbs, ssh,
device config pull|plan|apply): `EMERGENCY LOCKDOWN on scope 'site-a' since
<time> by <user> (<duration>): logins of groups <groups> are refused;
superusers keep access. Reason: <text>`. Also in `status`, `config
validate`, `scope|device|host show` and `list`, and `emergency lockdown
status`; `--json` keeps stdout unchanged and gains a `lockdown` object; the
exit status is unchanged. Engineers see it for their own scopes. No marker
in the prompt.
